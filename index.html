1984 by George Orwell

1984 by George Orwell is a classic novel that I have not finished yet, but it's incredibly mind-boggling. I got onto this book from a recommendation from my partner, and I am savouring it. The novel has incredible concepts and ideas that really warp and change one's idea of trust; the value of trust in this novel is the embodiment of giving your entire being, mind, and philosophy, which should be what it's about. However, 1984 challenges that simple definition of trust and displays it in a scenario where trust is survival to depict the true weight and value trust holds. This book has me enamoured, and I've annotated some sentences that I personally find mind-changing and interesting.

The novel is gruesome and holds truth. Essentially, it's about the main character, Winston, who lives in a society where every move, step, action, even thought is monitored. The society presents an illusion of choice; in reality, every little thing in the environment he's residing in influences his "choices", essentially controlling him and all who live in Oceania. Everyone is being watched, influenced, and controlled, giving a sense of hopelessness and presenting brainwashing that even if you realize people are being brainwashed and controlled, you can't escape the cycle or death; that there's nothing you can do, which causes people to conform to the society that wrecks the future, knowing that what you're doing is wrong (but even so, everyone around you says you're doing the right thing, so who's telling the truth, are you able to trust yourself or others?) The novel makes you question not only everything else but your entire being along with it. The government (which controls and created this environment in the society) has corrupted every single relationship there is, to your loved ones and even your children, making trust impossible.

The reason this is my favourite read at the moment is that it challenges concepts we think we know and understand, and retells them in an unrecognizable way. Not only that, but there are concepts in this novel that many, including me, find difficult to put into words, or have had that thought but completely forgot about the concept. It makes me revisit previous years, wondering how my mind changed, what couldve influenced it, and really weigh in on how one's mind grew up or dulled down mentally.

"Why then did that horror, which altered nothing, have to lie embedded in the future?" — This single line expresses how people who rebel against the Party (the government) ultimately go through this torture of hiding, confessing, self-doubt, thinking that they are a psychopath or thinking that everything and everyone around them is surveilling their every move, waiting for the rebel to perish a gruesome death that's inescapable because all who defy will be caught and slaughtered. Eventually, no matter what, this horror of grovelling and screaming for mercy, even after turning yourself in, will result in death. Simply because it's predetermined, once you think, you are predetermined to eventually die no matter what you do, even if you were to help make a change in the environment, open people's eyes to the manipulation and control they've been under, your fate is sealed because no one thing can change overnight. So this line is saying, if we go through this torture of tormenting ourselves mentally, through simply being alive in this hell of an environment where opinions lead to the world and mine own destruction, why go through that torture in the first place if it leads to the same future? Why torture ourselves knowing and anticipating a pain so great that even if we beg, nothing will change? What does this suffering equate to if the end result will always be the same?

And I thought that was interesting and brings in how suicide is viewed, yet regardless, people continue to rebel and hide and fight knowing that their fate is set. That speaks volumes.

This novel has dark and amazing concepts, which is why it's my favourite read :)
